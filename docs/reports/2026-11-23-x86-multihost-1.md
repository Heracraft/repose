## Findings in detail

### F1: one host per account

At 22:05 on box A, `repose host add e2e-x86-a` answered `Your plan takes one host. `repose host ls` lists what you have.`, exit 1. The slot was held by `e2e-mac` and by `e2e-x86-b`, which the conductor had made by mistake. After both were removed, A went in. With A online, `repose host add e2e-x86-b2` gave the same sentence, exit 1. The cause is in the code: `ExemptHosts = 1` (internal/billing/ownhardware.go) and the Own plan's `Hosts: 1`. billing.md says "for one host you own". So the limit is intended, not a defect. But the customer this program serves cannot buy two hosts, and no page says what a second host costs. After I-1582 (live 22:23:47) the exempt account took a second and a third host. Customers who pay are still limited to one. Instrument: the CLI against the live api. Falsifier: a paying Own account adding a second host. Not run, because I had no paying account.

### F2: you cannot choose a host

The pool had two hosts, both online and with equal room. Three runs of `repose run e2e-x86-pN --size small --host e2e-x86-a --temp -d` landed on a, b and a (p5, p6, p7), and a fourth landed on b (p8). Every run exited 0. p8's whole output: `✓ Created e2e-x86-p8 (small, temporary until Nov 24 22:31)  4.0s ... Ready in 6.5s.` It never names the host. With B offline, `--host e2e-x86-b` created e2e-x86-o2 on e2e-x86-a, also exit 0. On the dashboard, New project's **Runs on** `<select>` held `["e2e-x86-b", "repose's servers"]`. e2e-x86-a was missing. The conductor said this was known (I-1390, placement `own`). It is recorded here and was not hunted.

### F3: how much of the box is usable

Box A: `host ls --json` reported `vcpus: 12`, `cores: 6`, `mem_bytes: 66471268352`. Running on it were small, small and xl (6 vCPU, 24 GB). A second xl was refused: `repose has no room for this machine. A machine holds its memory while it runs, so stopping one frees room for it.`, exit 8, in 54 ms. A large and then a small were accepted (9 vCPU, 36 GB), and a tiny was refused (9.5). Each refusal fell exactly where the vCPU total passed 9, which is 6 cores × 150% (the scheduler's `cpuRoomSQL`). On the host at that moment, `free -g` showed 56 GiB available. On two boxes the pool stopped at 6 machines each (9 vCPU each). own-hardware.md promises "keeps an eighth of its memory, never less than 2 GB, for itself; the rest is what your machines may hold", which is about 54 GB here. The box has 12 threads, but the scheduler counts its 6 physical cores, so the customer gets two thirds of the promise. The refusal also uses the hosted wording ("repose has no room"), names no host, and with B offline does not say that B would have had room. Falsifier: a create that fits under the 9 vCPU line is refused, or one over it is accepted. Neither happened in 9 creates. Related: ENG-84, ENG-90, ENG-93, ENG-191.

### F4: the kernel warning

Both DAL installs printed `install-host: warning: kernel 6.8.0-149-generic is older than 6.18. Repose's own hosts run 6.18.57 or later for the fix to CVE-2026-99999 (a guest-to-host escape); use a kernel with that fix.`, and then completed (exit 0). own-hardware.md lists "Ubuntu 24.04, or Debian 12 or later" as what the box needs, and does not mention the warning. Stock 24.04 runs 6.8. So a careful customer is told, at install, that the box they were told to use is exposed, and is given no command to fix it. A customer who searches for the CVE id finds nothing public. The first NYC install's output was read only through `tail -40`, so I did not see whether the warning printed there.

### F5: giving the box back

At 22:37, on box B, `repose host rm e2e-x86-b --yes` exited 0. `systemctl is-active hostd` still said `active` 60 s later, and its journal logs `stream_disconnect ... PermissionDenied`, then `Unavailable`, as it retries. `vgs` showed `vg-guests` holding all 894 GB of the `--device` disk. `repose host rm --local --yes` on the box answered `There is no appliance on this Mac.`, exit 0. `install-host.sh` has no uninstall option (grep for uninstall and remove finds none), and own-hardware.md's "Taking it off" describes only the Mac. A trial customer who decides not to pay cannot leave cleanly. Related: ENG-175, where re-adding the same box fails.

### F6: what a customer sees while a box is away

These readings are from box A at 22:16 to 22:19 and box B at 22:32 to 22:36.
- `repose ls` prints `<machine>: your host e2e-x86-b has not answered for 2m.` once for each machine on the host: 5 lines for A, 6 for B.
- `repose start e2e-x86-2` on a stopped machine printed `Waiting for your host e2e-x86-a to come back... 30s` and was still waiting at my 60 s timeout. own-hardware.md says "`repose start` is refused while it is quiet".
- `repose exec` printed, after 15.4 s: `e2e-x86-1 is running but did not answer ssh in 15 s. Last error from ssh: e2e-x86-1 is on a host the control plane cannot reach right now; try again shortly; e2e-x86-1.user-c7fh26yzrl93@ssh.repose.run: Permission denied (publickey).` The first sentence is right. The tail tells the customer their key is wrong, and it is not.
- The time window, read on a clock: B showed `online` at +28 s after the network came back. `repose exec` failed (cannot reach) between about +30 and +44 s and worked at +47 s.
- A new run, refused, offers `repose run --host repose`. The Own plan sells nothing on repose's servers, so that advice cannot work for a customer who pays. Not measured, because the account is billing-exempt.

### F7: the join argument in the journal

`sudo journalctl -b | grep -F <argument>` found 1 hit, the `sudo` COMMAND line. `~/install.log` held 0 hits, and `ps` showed none after the install. The docs already warn that the argument is visible on the command line while the install runs. They do not say that sudo keeps a copy.

### F12: the late machine

The provider powered A off at 22:19:31 and on at 22:21:25. At +96 s, e2e-x86-1, -3 and -flarge were `starting` and e2e-x86-fsmall was `stopped`. The first three were `running` at +100 s, fsmall was `starting` at +141 s and `running` at +145 s. Its events: `22:23:01 machine stopped`, `22:23:01 server restarted ... it was back at 22:22 UTC`, `22:23:47 starting`, `22:23:50 running`. One observation; kept and marked unreproduced.

## Method and what could not run

- I ran everything as the customer would type it: the CLI from woker-1 (the "laptop") and on each box. The CLI on woker-1 was v0.1.61 in `~/lab/home/.local/bin`, with its own `XDG_CONFIG_HOME`, so the woker-1 CLI and its expired login were left alone. Sessions were minted with `~/briefs/mint-owner-cli-session.sh` (boxes) and with a local copy of the same logic (woker-1). No token or join argument was printed: the step was written to a 0600 file and run from there.
- I timed runs with `date +%s%N` around each command and watched states by polling `repose host ls` and `repose ls --json` every 3 to 5 s. Offline was made with `iptables -I OUTPUT -p udp --dport 51820 -j DROP`, with a `systemd-run` timer as a safety net to undo it. Power loss was made with the provider's power_off and power_on actions.
- Machines made: e2e-x86-1, 2, 3, flarge, fsmall (box A), and e2e-x86-p1 to p8, x1, x2, s9, s10, o1, o2 (two boxes). All were destroyed. e2e-x86-probe was refused and never existed. Hosts made and removed: e2e-x86-a (twice), e2e-x86-b, e2e-x86-c. Also removed: the stale `e2e-mac` and the conductor's stray `e2e-x86-b` (at 22:06, with the conductor's agreement). `repose host ls` at the end: `You have no hardware of your own.` No non-e2e project was touched or landed on a test host.
- The first box A was removed through the dead-host path (power off, then `repose host rm` of the offline host, then `repose rm` of each stranded machine) shortly before the limit was lifted. So the two-box half ran on a fresh pair of boxes. The second A's install took 64 s, against 13 s for the first.
- `repose run` copied woker-1's `gh` and `git` logins into the test machines, as documented. The machines are destroyed.
- Not run: Claude Code, Codex and Gemini past a prompt (no logins). Interactive work inside `repose attach` beyond connecting and detaching. The plan gates, because the account is billing-exempt, so F1's "paying account" falsifier is open. Debian. A VM with nested virtualisation. A host on Intel. Unattended updates over days. The browser view and triggers. `host add --json`, which is ENG-102 and would print a key. Moving machines, which has no command.
- Evidence rules that bit me. A `script -c "timeout 12 repose attach"` did not end at 12 s. I moved to a private tmux server (`tmux -L x86lab`) and did not file it, because I could not tell my harness from the CLI. Also, my first reboot watcher folded identical lines together and ended early. I re-ran it with change-only logging and kept the second run's figures.
